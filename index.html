Presentation

Why generative MEF?

From blending exposures to generating missing content

Multi-exposure fusion (MEF) combines differently exposed images into a single result with a wider usable luminance range. In real scenes, however, exposure brackets can contain large motion, occlusion, and severe saturation.

Conventional MEF is efficient and preserves observed pixels well, but it often struggles when correspondences break or image content is completely missing. In those cases, simple alignment and blending are not enough: the missing structure must be synthesized.

Diffusion-based generative MEF provides a strong prior for this problem, but existing approaches process high-resolution images patch by patch. This creates two practical limitations: high computational cost and structural uncertainty in saturated regions where guidance is weak.

Extreme dynamic range

Large saturated regions may contain no directly recoverable image evidence.

Dynamic motion

Moving objects and occlusions make deterministic alignment and blending unreliable.

High resolution

Patch-wise diffusion scales poorly when the target image becomes large.

Core idea

Generate globally, refine locally

Our key observation is that the expensive generative prior does not need to operate at the final resolution. Diffusion is most valuable for resolving global exposure inconsistencies, motion, occlusion, and missing content. Once the global structure is established, high-resolution details can be recovered from the original exposure pair using a lightweight coordinate-based fusion function.

LIIFusion therefore separates generative MEF into two complementary stages: a low-resolution generative coarse stage and a high-resolution implicit fine stage.

1. Coarse-to-fine Generative MEF

A new generative MEF paradigm that moves diffusion to low resolution and performs high-resolution refinement separately.

2. INR as a Fusion Function

We reformulate LIIF from a super-resolution decoder into a coordinate-conditioned multi-exposure fusion function.

3. Practical Generative Fusion

Adaptive exposure correction improves structural reliability in saturated regions, while the full framework achieves up to 3.5× faster inference than prior generative MEF.

Method

LIIFusion pipeline

Stage 1

Coarse fusion: use diffusion where generation matters

The over-exposed (OE) and under-exposed (UE) inputs are first downsampled. A diffusion-based generative MEF model then performs fusion near its native operating resolution, allowing the model to reason over the global scene context without expensive high-resolution patch sampling.

This stage handles the parts of MEF that benefit most from a generative prior: exposure inconsistencies, motion artifacts, occlusions, and regions where image content must be synthesized.

Whole-scene context

Coarse fusion sees the downsampled scene globally instead of independently synthesizing many high-resolution patches.

Minimal diffusion usage

The expensive sampling stage is restricted to the low-resolution representation.

Adaptive Exposure Correction

Preserve structure before generative fusion

Low-resolution fusion is efficient, but severe over-exposure can erase fine structure before the diffusion model sees it. In saturated regions, weak fidelity guidance can cause the coarse model to generate blurred or distorted structures.

Adaptive Exposure Correction (AEC) estimates the luminance difference between OE and UE inputs and selectively attenuates excessively bright OE regions. The corrected OE image provides a more reliable structural cue during generative fusion.

Before AEC

Saturated signs, maps, and bright textures can collapse at low resolution.

After AEC

The coarse result retains stronger structural cues for the subsequent fine stage.

Stage 2

Fine fusion: reformulating LIIF for multi-exposure fusion

The fine stage is not a conventional super-resolution module. It receives features from the coarse fused image together with features extracted from the original high-resolution OE and UE inputs.

For each target coordinate, a lightweight implicit decoder predicts the final RGB value using both global coarse context and local high-resolution exposure evidence. In other words, LIIF is adapted from an image representation function into a multi-exposure conditional fusion function.

Because prediction is coordinate-based, the same learned function can be queried at different target resolutions without requiring a fixed integer upsampling scale.

Coarse feature

Provides globally coherent generative context.

OE / UE features

Recover high-frequency details from the original exposures.

Continuous query

Produces pixel-wise fusion at arbitrary target coordinates.

Human preference study

Users prefer the coarse-to-fine result

We additionally evaluate perceptual preference with 22 participants. Participants rank four anonymized MEF results. LIIFusion receives the highest best-choice rate and the best average rank, while remaining much faster than UltraFusion.

This result supports the central goal of LIIFusion: reducing the computational burden of generative MEF without sacrificing the perceptual advantages of generative fusion.

Takeaway

A practical path toward generative MEF

LIIFusion decouples generation from high-resolution reconstruction. Diffusion is used at low resolution to solve the hard global ambiguities of MEF, while an implicit fusion function efficiently brings back high-resolution evidence from the original exposures.

The resulting framework preserves the advantages of generative MEF— natural synthesis, robustness to large motion, and completion of saturated regions— while substantially improving high-resolution efficiency and structural detail.

Global generation

Resolve motion, saturation, and missing content at low resolution.

Local detail fusion

Recover HR information directly from OE and UE inputs.

Paper

Abstract

Multi-exposure fusion (MEF) expands the luminance range beyond what a single exposure can capture. Combining images taken at different exposure levels requires handling geometric differences while naturally merging their complementary brightness information. It often demands generative completion where details are missing. Diffusion-based generative methods address these challenges; however, they are computationally expensive and struggle to preserve fine structures in saturated regions. We propose LIIFusion, a coarse-to-fine framework that balances fusion quality and efficiency in generative MEF. The coarse stage performs low-resolution generative fusion, enhanced by adaptive exposure correction that recovers structure lost in saturated over-exposed areas. The fine stage adapts a local implicit image function into a multi-exposure fusion function: conditioned on the high-resolution OE/UE sources and the coarse output, it queries arbitrary target coordinates and fuses source evidence regardless of the input resolution. LIIFusion achieves up to 3.5× speed-up over existing generative methods while maintaining or improving structural fidelity and perceptual quality.
